The most effective strategy in Austria is to combine medium‑term rental platforms, local classifieds, and non‑hotel lodging networks, while checking city‑specific rules—especially in Vienna, where short‑term rentals face strict limits.

🧭 1. Start with platforms that specialize in non‑hotel short stays These are the most reliable for stays from a few weeks to a few months.

  • Airbnb (but with restrictions in Vienna) — Vienna limits whole‑unit rentals to 90 days/year without a permit, and commercial short‑term letting in residential zones is prohibited. This means supply is lower but still exists in permitted zones.
  • Booking.com apartments — Many private apartments list here for short stays; less impacted by Vienna’s zoning rules if they operate legally.
  • Flatio — Focuses on medium‑term furnished rentals (1–6 months). Often used by digital nomads and avoids the strictest STR regulations.
  • HousingAnywhere — Good for furnished sublets, especially in university cities.

Why this works: These platforms filter for legal, furnished, short‑term units and reduce the risk of illegal STRs, which Vienna actively investigates.

🧭 2. Use Austrian local marketplaces for private sublets These are excellent for non‑touristic, cheaper options.

  • Willhaben.at — Austria’s largest classifieds site; search for Zwischenmiete, Untermiete, befristet.
  • WG‑Gesucht.at — Not only for rooms; many full flats appear as temporary sublets.
  • Facebook Marketplace / expat groups — Useful but requires more due diligence.

Why this works: Many Austrians prefer subletting privately rather than listing on STR platforms due to regulatory complexity.

🧭 3. Consider non‑hotel lodging networks These are ideal if you want something more “home‑like” without entering the regulated STR market.

  • Farm stays (Urlaub am Bauernhof) — Rural, authentic, often available for short stays.
  • Pensionen & Gästehäuser — Family‑run guesthouses that are not hotels but operate legally.
  • Monteurzimmer / worker rooms — Simple, furnished rooms for short‑term workers; widely available outside Vienna.

🧭 4. Understand the regulatory landscape (critical in Austria) Austria—especially Vienna—has tightened STR rules:

  • Vienna:
    • Whole‑unit STRs limited to 90 days/year unless you obtain an exemption permit requiring co‑owner consent and building‑wide limits.
    • Commercial STRs in residential zones are prohibited.
    • Illegal listings can lead to fines up to €50,000.
  • Other regions (Salzburg, Tyrol, Styria):
    Regulations exist but are generally less restrictive than Vienna.

Implication for your search:
If you’re looking in Vienna, prioritize medium‑term platforms (Flatio, HousingAnywhere) and legal guesthouses. In other regions, Airbnb and private sublets are more abundant.

🧭 5. A practical search workflow (optimized for Austria) 1. Define your duration:
- <30 days → guesthouses, pensions, legal STRs
- 1–6 months → Flatio, HousingAnywhere, Willhaben
2. Check zoning if in Vienna (STR restrictions are strict).
3. Cross‑check the address on Vienna’s zoning map (if applicable).
4. Ask for Meldezettel possibility if you need registration (not always possible in STRs).
5. Verify legality: ask whether the landlord has the required permit (Vienna only).

  1. Dedicated furnished-apartment providers (high reliability, flexible durations) These are purpose‑built for temporary stays and are often used by companies, contractors, and relocators.

  • NiceFlats Villach — Fully furnished apartments with all‑inclusive pricing, kitchens, and flexible terms; designed for short‑term business stays. They explicitly position themselves as a step beyond Airbnb and offer professional invoicing and turnkey service.
    Best for: 2 weeks → several months, predictable costs, corporate‑style reliability.

  • Kurzzeitmiete (KURZZEiTmiete) — A major Austrian platform for Wohnen auf Zeit (temporary living). Their Klagenfurt listings include studios and multi‑room apartments, all furnished, with transparent pricing for the entire stay.
    Best for: 1–6 months, furnished units with clear contracts.

These two sources alone cover a large share of legal, stable, non‑hotel options in Carinthia.

  1. Short‑term apartments via curated platforms These are more “holiday‑apartment” oriented but still suitable for multi‑week stays.
